## Summary

Adam Harris, Gabby Deutch, and David Graham examined the final stretch of the election, centering on Michigan’s Senate race after Haley Stevens, the Democratic primary loser to Abdul El-Sayed, endorsed Republican Mike Rogers and appeared with him at church following a contentious ad from an outside group tying grocery prices to U.S. military support for Israel. They discussed late-October polling from Fox News, the Financial Times, and CNBC showing corruption atop voters’ issue lists, alongside affordability, health care, and the Iran war; Deutch reported Pennsylvania and Ohio voters, including two 21- and 22-year-old Trump voters at East Stroudsburg University, expressing anger over perceived lies about wars and costs, including a fraternity brother deployed to the Middle East. Named figures and races included Josh Shapiro’s reported 25-point Pennsylvania lead, Paige Cognetti vs. Rob Bresnahan, Janelle Stelson vs. Scott Perry, El-Sayed vs. Rogers, Jocelyn Benson, Sherrod Brown, Roy Cooper, Trump’s Kansas and Iowa rallies and reflecting-pool closing pitch, and bellwether Senate races involving Chris Pappas, Annie Andrews, and Darlene Graham. The discussion also covered Epstein files, hidden donations to a White House ballroom, congressional stock-trading bans, and possible efforts to reverse Trump-era Medicare and Medicaid cuts.

Actionable follow-up: if tracking political or policy outcomes, watch whether Stevens’ endorsement shifts Michigan turnout among Jewish and pro-Israel Democrats, whether Benson’s stronger polling helps El-Sayed, and whether Pennsylvania swing seats flip; use New Hampshire and South Carolina Senate margins as early indicators, but avoid overreacting to early returns as in 2018. For research or civic decisions, prioritize corruption-related proposals with plausible bipartisan paths, especially a congressional stock-trading ban, and track whether Democrats pursue investigations, impeachment threats, or concrete legislative reversals of health-care cuts. Health implications center on Medicare and Medicaid reductions and potential coverage losses, while cost-of-living promises—gas prices, groceries, tariffs—and Iran-war-related deployments are key voter concerns. Follow-up questions: Can anger sustain a governing coalition through 2026 and 2028? Will divided government produce policy or only investigations? Which health-care cuts are realistically reversible?
