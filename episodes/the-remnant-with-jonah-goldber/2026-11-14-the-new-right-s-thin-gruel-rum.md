Jonah Goldberg, on The Remnant/Dispatch, revisited his controversy over Turning Point USA and Charlie Kirk, saying Kirk’s murder was heinous and grotesque, praising Kirk’s wife’s grace, noting it had been 14 months, and denying he was “dancing on Charlie Kirk’s grave” despite attacks from Chris Ruffo and Larry O’Connor; he also called Dave Chappelle’s “glad he was murdered” line gross. He argued that much post-election right-wing blame—on Iran, bad advisers, the Lincoln Memorial speech, or Senate Majority Leader John Thune’s failure to pass the SAVE Act/abolish the filibuster—is a “bad boy are thing” excuse that avoids criticizing Trump. He claimed Thune lacked votes and Senate power to force the SAVE Act, that the SAVE Act was a proof-of-citizenship measure rather than a simple voter-ID law, and that it could burden low-propensity voters, people without passports/birth certificates, and women with changed names. He said voter-ID laws are popular and reasonable, but there is no evidence of millions of illegal immigrants voting; he cited known cases in the mid-to-high hundreds, possibly around a thousand or 10,000, and a New Jersey case as bureaucratic error. He tied Republican losses to Trump fatigue, Iran, corruption, and the perception of vote suppression, while naming Kevin Roberts, Molly Hemingway, Vance, Oren Cass, Rick Scott, Mike Lee, and Putin as part of the surrounding narrative.
