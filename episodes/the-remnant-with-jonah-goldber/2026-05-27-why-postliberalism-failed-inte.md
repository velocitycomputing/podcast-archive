## Summary

Jonah Goldberg hosts authors James M. Patterson and Thomas D. Howes to discuss their book "Why Postliberalism Failed," examining the intellectual movement that Goldberg has publicly criticized. The conversation covers postliberalism's core concepts and variants—including integralism, national conservatism, and the influence of figures like Adrian Vermeule and Patrick Deneen—as well as related phenomena such as elite capture, Viktor Orbán's governance model, right-wing Hegelianism, and the role of institutions like ISI and the Heritage Foundation. The episode also addresses concerning undercurrents including ground-level antisemitism, authoritarian thinking, and an appetite for political violence within postliberal circles.

To deepen your understanding, consult the provided show notes, which link directly to key texts: Vermeule's Atlantic essay "Beyond Originalism" and his other pieces, Deneen's "Why Liberalism Failed," and the integralism book. These resources offer firsthand arguments from the movement's own thinkers, allowing you to assess whether postliberalism's critique of liberalism has merit independent of Goldberg's skepticism. The episode provides sufficient intellectual scaffolding to then engage with these primary sources critically.
