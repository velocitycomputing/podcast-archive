## Summary

Dr Robert Pape, returning to Triggernometry, argues the United States is in an escalation trap in Iran after the February 28 US-Israeli bombing, claiming Iran survived, gained selective control over Hormuz shipping, and shifted bargaining to its terms, with an April 8 ceasefire and a June 17 memorandum of understanding that he says he predicted would fail and that was broken within days; he says the administration moved from regime-change bombing to a long air campaign to an economic blockade, all historically weak strategies, and that the conflict is now in its ninth month with worldwide economic consequences, citing the WWI British blockade killing 700,000 German civilians without forcing German concession. He contrasts the 1991 Gulf War’s 43-day, 147-US-death model, which he says led Europe and China to cut military budgets and armed forces, with the “second precision revolution” of cheap drones, noting Shahed 136 drones cost roughly $20,000-$50,000 versus $35 million US drones, and that Houthis shot down seven US drones and two F-18s during a 52-day spring 2025 campaign; he also claims historically high US support for political violence, citing 40 million Americans supporting use of the military, ICE, or National Guard to suppress democratic protesters and 60 million supporting force to prevent Trump from being president, and warns November-December midterms plus January could be a major stress test for American democracy, with Ted Cruz’s warning about the war lasting into the midterms noted.

No direct health implications are supplied; actionable follow-ups are to monitor concrete escalation-trap indicators: whether Iran maintains selective control of Hormuz, whether blockade or bombing produces Iranian concessions, whether any off-ramp is taken, whether the June 17 MOU or ceasefire holds, and whether energy prices, shipping insurance, and supply-chain costs rise. For research, Pape’s framework points to studying strategic air campaigns and blockades since World War I, the 1991 Gulf War model’s obsolescence, civilian drone diffusion, Ukraine’s donor-funded drone factories around 2023, and the Chicago Project on Security and Threats; decisions to consider include reviewing personal or business exposure to fuel, diesel, fertilizer, and maritime trade volatility, and preparing for heightened political-violence risk around the midterms and January. Follow-up questions should ask what specific off-ramps remain, what classified briefing documents reveal about military warnings, how the 40 million and 60 million political-violence figures are measured, whether civilian leaders understood strategy versus tactics, and whether cheap drone proliferation changes deterrence for the US, Europe, and China.
