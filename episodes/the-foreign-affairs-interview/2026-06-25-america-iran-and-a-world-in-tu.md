## Summary

The episode examines how the Iran war's conclusion exposes fundamental forces reshaping global power dynamics, including unconstrained leaders willing to deploy military force, economic leverage as a strategic tool, destabilizing technologies that disrupt decision-making and development models, and the fracturing of traditional alliances alongside emerging geopolitical alignments. Bremmer, president of the Eurasia Group, explores how these dynamics create conditions of weaponized power and systemic risk across the international system.

For actionable understanding, monitor three interconnected trends: (1) leadership behavior in nuclear and near-nuclear states that prioritizes military gambles over diplomacy, (2) shifts in economic sanctions and trade relationships as leverage tools, and (3) how AI and emerging technologies are reshaping geopolitical calculations and destabilizing conventional strategic planning. Watch for signs of alliance fracturing among traditional partners (particularly NATO, Gulf states) and new bloc formation as these pressures intensify.

## Transcript

The war in Iran may have come to an end, but both the course and the conclusion of that war have brought into sharp relief the forces that increasingly define a world of weaponized power and systemic risk: unconstrained leaders willing to gamble with military force; the search for, and use of, economic leverage; technologies destabilizing both decision-making and development models; and old alliances fracturing and new alignments forming.
Ian Bremmer is a leading geopolitical analyst and the president and founder of the Eurasia Group. His most recent piece for Foreign Affairs is about the long-term consequences of the Iran war, but he has also written on shifts in global power, the effect of technology on geopolitics, and much else. Dan Kurtz-Phelan spoke to Bremmer on Monday, June 22, about how to make sense of today’s global turmoil.
