## Summary

Sam Harris and Siddhartha Mukherjee explore contemporary cancer science across multiple dimensions: whether cancer constitutes one disease or many, barriers to prevention, inflammation and air pollution as carcinogenic factors, the debunking of the cell phone–cancer link, diagnostic advances like liquid biopsies paired with Bayesian reasoning, therapeutic progress in immunotherapy and CAR-T cells, drug pricing disparities, and AI's emerging role in drug discovery. The conversation also examines the broader state of American medical science and updated insights from Mukherjee's revised edition of *The Emperor of All Maladies*.

For someone managing personal health: prioritize reducing exposure to confirmed environmental carcinogens (air pollution, chronic inflammation) over unfounded risks like cell phones; familiarize yourself with liquid biopsy technology as an emerging early-detection tool; and understand that immunotherapy and CAR-T approaches now represent real treatment options rather than experimental last resorts. If you or a family member has cancer history, ask your physician whether you're a candidate for these newer monitoring or therapeutic approaches, and stay informed about AI-accelerated drug discoveries entering trials that might offer alternatives to standard pricing regimens.

## Transcript

Sam Harris speaks with Siddhartha Mukherjee about the science of cancer. They discuss the updated edition of The Emperor of All Maladies, whether cancer is one disease or many, why prevention is so hard, inflammation and air pollution as carcinogens, the myth that cell phones cause cancer, liquid biopsies and Bayesian reasoning, immunotherapy and CAR T cells, drug pricing, the promise of AI in drug discovery, the state of American medical science, and other topics.
Siddhartha Mukherjee is the author of The Song of the Cell; The Gene: An Intimate History, a #1 New York Times bestseller; and The Laws of Medicine. He is a Rhodes scholar, an associate professor of medicine at Columbia University, and a cancer physician and researcher. He has published articles in many journals, including Nature, Cell, The New York Times Magazine, The New Yorker, and others.
