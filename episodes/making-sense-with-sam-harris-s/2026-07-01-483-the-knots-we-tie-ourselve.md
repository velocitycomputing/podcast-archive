## Summary

Sam Harris interviews Alain de Botton, a writer and psychotherapist, to explore the psychological and existential roots of unhappiness and modern discontent. The conversation covers how secular society dismisses ecstasy and ritual despite their psychological value, psychedelics as instruments for self-discovery, Freud's continuing relevance, artificial intelligence as a mirror for self-understanding, critiques of meritocracy's psychological toll, the trap of performative purity in cancel culture, and how we psychologically relate to mortality.

De Botton's work emphasizes emotional education as essential for managing relationships and making better choices. For the listener, the actionable implication is to examine which "knots" you've tied yourself into—unexamined assumptions about achievement, belonging, purity, or happiness—and consider whether secular dismissal of ritual and introspective tools (including psychedelic-assisted therapy if available) might offer genuine psychological relief rather than indulgence.

## Transcript

Sam Harris speaks with Alain de Botton about the psychology of unhappiness, the secular world's discomfort with ecstasy and ritual, psychedelics as a tool for self-discovery, Freud's legacy, AI as the ultimate mirror, the case against meritocracy, the cancel culture purity trap, the psychological meaning of death, and other topics.
Alain de Botton is a writer, psychotherapist, and founder of The School of Life. He is interested in art, ideas, and the need for emotional education to help us manage relationships and make better choices.
