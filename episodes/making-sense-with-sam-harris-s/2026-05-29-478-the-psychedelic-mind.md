## Summary

Sam Harris speaks with neuroscientist Robin Carhart-Harris about the current state of psychedelic research and therapeutic applications. Their discussion covers the FDA's recent denial of MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD treatment, the mechanisms by which psychedelics affect the brain (including the REBUS model and effects on the default mode network and ego dissolution), the critical importance of set and setting in psychedelic experiences, risks for vulnerable populations, microdosing practices, the neuroscience of consciousness, and reported encounters with DMT entities. Carhart-Harris draws on his extensive research designing brain imaging studies with LSD, psilocybin, MDMA, and DMT, as well as clinical trials for treating severe depression, anorexia, and fibromyalgia.

For individuals interested in psychedelics, the episode underscores that therapeutic outcomes depend heavily on psychological preparation and environmental context—not just the substance itself. Anyone considering psychedelic use should understand that vulnerable populations face elevated risks, and that microdosing remains an area of ongoing research rather than established practice. The discussion also highlights that psychedelic-assisted therapy is progressing through clinical trials, though FDA approval remains limited and contested, making access through legitimate research protocols or clinical settings essential rather than self-experimentation.

## Transcript

Sam Harris speaks with Robin Carhart-Harris about psychedelic research and its therapeutic potential. They discuss the current state of the field, the FDA denial of MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD, the critical role of set and setting, risks for vulnerable populations, the REBUS model of how psychedelics work on the brain, the default mode network and ego dissolution, microdosing, the neuroscience of consciousness, DMT entities, and other topics.
Dr Carhart-Harris moved to Imperial College London in 2008 after obtaining a PhD in Psychopharmacology from the University of Bristol. He has designed human brain imaging studies with LSD, psilocybin, MDMA, and DMT, and several clinical trials of psilocybin therapy for severe mental illnesses, including 3 key trials in depression plus 2 further trials in anorexia and fibromyalgia syndrome. Dr Carhart-Harris founded the Centre for Psychedelic Research at Imperial College London in April 2019, the first of its kind in the world. In 2020, he was named among the top 31 medical scientists by The Times newspaper. Dr Carhart-Harris became the inaugural Ralph Metzner Distinguished Professor of Neurology and Psychiatry at the University of California, San Francisco in 2021, and in the same year, was listed in TIME magazine’s ‘100 Next’ - a list of emerging leaders from around the world who are shaping the future.
