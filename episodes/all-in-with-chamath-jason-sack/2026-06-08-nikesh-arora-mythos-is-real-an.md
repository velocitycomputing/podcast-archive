## Summary

Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora claimed "analytical SaaS is dead" as AI disrupts the market, arguing that traditional analytics tools won't survive the AI wave. He highlighted Claude Mythos discovering years of vulnerabilities in Palo Alto's code within weeks, raising concerns about cyber defenders losing the race against AI attackers. Arora questioned where revenue would come from if AI models become a utility, suggesting money shifts to applications built on top of foundational models. He rated Waymo, Google, and OpenAI as key players, emphasizing Google's potential to reach a $10T valuation through AI integration and stating Palo Alto's M&A strategy aims for a $1T market cap.

Businesses should immediately reassess SaaS investments, prioritizing AI-native solutions over legacy analytical tools to avoid obsolescence. Security teams must integrate AI vulnerability scanning like Claude Mythos into their testing cycles, not just traditional methods, to address rapid threat discovery. Investors should evaluate tech giants' AI integration depth (not just AI spending) for growth potential, particularly Google's path to $10T, and monitor Palo Alto's M&A activity as a signal for consolidation in AI security. Follow-up questions should focus on specific AI security testing frameworks and how to measure AI utility monetization beyond the model layer.
