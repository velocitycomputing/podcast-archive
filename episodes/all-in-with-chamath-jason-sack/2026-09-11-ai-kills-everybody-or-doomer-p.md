## Summary

This episode of All-In (Chamath, Jason, Sacks, Friedberg) centered on a viral resignation post by Jacob Coxin, a researcher who left Anthropic after about six weeks, having previously worked at OpenAI. He said the people building AI believe it could kill everyone by the end of the decade and that both labs are gambling with lives in a race toward self-improving superintelligence. Anthropic's head of alignment science, Evan Hubinger, endorsed the post and put the odds of AI killing all humans at over 10% within a decade. The two posts drew roughly 200 million views combined, and Bernie Sanders and Governor JB Pritzker cited them in support of a superintelligence ban, a pause, and tighter AI regulation. Sacks called it a coordinated doomer operation. He said Coxin's account had no prior posts or followers, and that Encode AI, the AI Policy Network and the AI Futures Project amplified it within 15 minutes. He added that all three groups are funded by Jaan Tallinn, an Anthropic Series A co-lead, and that the Wall Street Journal appears to have been briefed under embargo. He also noted that Coxin canceled his appearance on the show. Sacks and Chamath argued that Anthropic faces an IPO contradiction: it cannot ask public investors to value it in the trillions while its own safety lead calls its core product potentially civilization-ending, which they said invites product-liability and disclosure problems, and they compared it to the tobacco companies. Friedberg compared the panic to past scares over climate change, COVID and Three Mile Island. He argued that recursive self-improvement needs only power, chips and an internet connection, so US restrictions would just let other countries pull ahead. All of them said the real goal is a federal AI regulator (an "FDA for AI") that would centralize control and eventually squeeze out open-source models, which can't be centrally monitored or rolled back. Sacks said such a regulator could also steer AI answers the way government pressure shaped social media during COVID. Chamath offered three explanations: the doomers are right, they sincerely believe it and are wrong (his leading theory, a kind of psychosis), or it is a coordinated campaign. Sacks answered that it can be an organized PR push without every participant being in on it. The material was cut off partway through, so the later topics in the title (OpenAI's math breakthrough, Nike) were not covered. Note that these are the hosts' claims and allegations. The transcript offers no independent evidence for the coordination or funding claims, and Coxin and Anthropic are not heard from.

Concrete things to monitor are Anthropic's S-1 and IPO disclosures, specifically how it words catastrophic-risk and liability language and whether it distances itself from or backs Coxin and Hubinger. Also watch Sanders's superintelligence-ban bill and any federal AI-regulator proposals, particularly any standards that would apply to open-weight models. If you build on or invest in AI, the open-source regulatory-risk argument is worth tracking. Friedberg's point is that rules requiring central monitoring and rollback could effectively exclude open-weight models. If you hold or consider Anthropic-linked exposure, the IPO-timing and liability angle is the most decision-relevant part.
