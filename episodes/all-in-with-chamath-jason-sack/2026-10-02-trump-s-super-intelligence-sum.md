## Summary

At Trump's White House Super Intelligence Summit, the president convened AI leaders (Elon Musk, Jensen Huang, Mark Zuckerberg, Dario Amodei, Sundar Pichai, Satya Nadella) to negotiate the White House Accord on Super Intelligence. The accord establishes mandatory safety governance without new legislation: companies accept responsibility for AI safety, implement internal controls verified by external auditors (professional firms like EY, not NGOs), report findings to independent board committees with fiduciary duty to act, and remain subject to FTC/SEC enforcement. The accord includes three practical infrastructure requirements: end-to-end traceability of AI deployments, mapping of AI use to organizational policies and risks, and auditable evidence demonstrating compliance—all deployable immediately. Saxby Friedberg noted that EY and his firm 8090 are already building this audit infrastructure and rolling it out to all major companies.

For the user: If your organization deploys AI systems, boards will soon face mandatory external audits requiring demonstrable governance and control. Begin now by creating traceability records of how AI is deployed against your standard operating procedures, documenting risks and policies, and centralizing auditable evidence for regulators, auditors, or lawsuits. Additionally, expect cyber defense to become AI-driven and board-priority within 12–18 months—treat it as a competitive necessity tied to national security, not optional compliance. Finally, if you're in infrastructure, finance, or energy, prepare for government allocation of compute resources and data center capacity as policy-critical utilities; this creates government procurement opportunities.
