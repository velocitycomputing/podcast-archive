## Summary

Andre Jick argues that a global debt spiral is underway, claiming inflation is not solely caused by the Iran war but by years of fiscal and monetary recklessness, roughly $2 trillion in annual deficit spending, and capital being pulled into AI; he cites $365 trillion in global debt, U.S. debt around $40 trillion growing by about $82,000 per second or $6 billion per day, annual interest above $1 trillion, and Trump’s Time Magazine remark that inflation can pay off debt. He says lenders are demanding higher real yields, pushing the U.S. 10-year to its highest since 2002, Britain’s 30-year above 6% for the first time since 1998, and Japan to record highs, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s “I am the house” challenge failed as bond buyers demanded more, with Treasury buying only $2–6 billion against a $32 trillion market. The material also links higher borrowing costs to a U.S. housing slowdown not seen since 1995, 7.3% mortgage rates, AI stocks up 9% versus non-AI stocks down 5.4% since August 24, and contrasts China’s falling 10-year yield of 1.7% with U.S. yields above 5%, attributing China’s low rates partly to electricity growth and partly to Evergrande’s collapse, falling home prices, deflation, and balance-sheet repair; interspersed ad segments promote Shaq’s Carvana spot, Taylor Brands, Ketone IQ, Quince, Perplexity Computer, and Ground News.

Monitor concrete indicators—10-year Treasury and mortgage rates, UK/Japan yields, China’s 10-year yield, AI versus non-AI market breadth, and Fed or Treasury moves toward yield-curve control—rather than relying on single-cause narratives about oil or war. Financially, stress-test household budgets against higher borrowing costs, prioritize high-interest variable debt, avoid assuming home prices or asset prices are protected, and if considering an LLC, business banking, or sponsor tools such as Taylor Brands, Perplexity, or Ground News, compare costs and verify claims independently; if considering Ketone IQ, treat the focus claims as marketing, check ingredients and medical interactions, and consult a clinician if diabetic, pregnant, on medication, or managing health conditions. Useful follow-up questions are whether inflation is being used to erode debt, whether the Fed will monetize Treasuries, whether China’s low yields reflect strength or deflation, and whether U.S. policy can restore manufacturing and housing supply enough to reduce reliance on financialized bailouts.
