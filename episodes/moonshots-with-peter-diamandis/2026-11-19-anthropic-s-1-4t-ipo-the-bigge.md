## Summary

Anthropic’s expected IPO under ticker ANT, with 825 million shares marketed at $70-$78, roughly $64 billion raised and a $1.4 trillion valuation, anchors a Moonshots discussion among Peter Diamandis, Alex, Dave Blunden, and Salim Ismail about AI wealth concentration, frontier-lab competition, and geopolitical power. The transcript claims Q3 revenue of $17 billion, up $1.4 billion year over year, an $81 billion annualized run rate as of October 31, 5x share demand, Wall Street expectations near $80, and $26 billion of non-voting shares from seven companies including Google, NVIDIA, AMD, and Microsoft, while Anthropic’s seven co-founders pledge 80% of personal equity to donation. Related data includes San Francisco’s 800,000 residents minting thousands of $10 million-plus fortunes, an average Anthropic employee holding about $72 million, OpenAI insiders at about $126 million, San Francisco absorbing more than a third of global venture capital, and time from founding to unicorn status falling from 21 years in 2000 to about 1 year now. Treat the material as a due-diligence checklist rather than a buy signal: verify Anthropic’s filing, final price, lockup terms, share count, revenue run rate, and the $26 billion anchor investment before considering exposure, and watch the six-month lockup, post-IPO trading, and whether employee liquidity floods the market or gets reinvested into AI startups. Research leads include NVIDIA’s board/activist-investor trajectory, CUDA versus custom inference chips, alleged circular financing, Google’s TPU expansion and Gemini 4 in the next 90-120 days, and the compressed unicorn timeline’s effect on venture funding; decisions to consider include diversifying away from concentrated AI narratives, timing any founder sprint around new angel capital, and separating paper wealth from durable cash flow. No direct health implications are present in the supplied material; the only health-adjacent action is to limit AI-market news consumption if it causes stress or sleep disruption, while follow-up questions should focus on whether Anthropic’s lockup release creates selling pressure, whether NVIDIA’s successor preserves CUDA demand, and whether Google’s quiet TPU buildout materially shifts frontier-lab infrastructure.
