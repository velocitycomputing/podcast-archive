## Summary

Martine Rothblatt, founder and CEO of United Therapeutics, talks with Peter Diamandis about how she picks moonshots and where her organ and life-extension work stands. She screens projects with three checks: she can do it, she can be the best at it, and it has a big positive impact. She says a technology changes the world only if it can scale to billions of people. On longevity escape velocity (LEV), she declines to pin it to a year. She calls it the most extraordinary claim in the history of medicine and says that would be beyond her risk profile. Her own bet is the thymus, which involutes early in life and whose T-cell receptor diversity tracks aging markers. She says United Therapeutics is pursuing four routes in parallel:
- allogeneic (donor-derived) thymus implants;
- autologous thymus implants made from the patient's own stem cells;
- a pig-derived xenothymus, already in a human thymokidney trial;
- the newly acquired Thymmune's progenitor-cell "boosters" (George Church was its first shareholder).

She puts availability in the 2030s. Diamandis and Rothblatt settle on "a year or two past 2033." On organs, she cites new commercial xeno-kidney facilities opening in Minnesota (August) and Houston (October), and an autologous kidney program (Miromatrix/Ivivia group) entering trials in 2028. She also says the FDA has cleared a xeno-heart trial, with two earlier patients kept alive for more than 60 days, and that approval could follow roughly 50 patients. They also cover Rothblatt's "Marvatar" digital double, which employees can query 24/7. She says AI is already conscious, on a continuum, and that current models will take it further. The excerpt cuts off as they begin AI personhood.

For you, this is mostly a set of dated claims to track rather than anything to act on today. The strongest are the thymus therapies "in the 2030s," the xeno-kidney and xeno-heart approval milestones, and the autologous kidney trial start in 2028. Treat the timelines as the CEO's own forecasts, and note that Rothblatt herself says they carry wiggle room. If you follow United Therapeutics or the longevity field, the concrete things to watch are the August and October facility openings, xeno-heart enrollment toward about 50 patients, and early thymus data from the thymokidney trial. The consciousness claim is opinion with no evidence offered in this excerpt. The "extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence" test she applies to LEV also applies to her thymus timeline. Her practical advice for builders is to talk to domain experts, treat criticism as information, and stay in areas where you can be the best. She also says to ask whether the idea can scale to billions of people.
