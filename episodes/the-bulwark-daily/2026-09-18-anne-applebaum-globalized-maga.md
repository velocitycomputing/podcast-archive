## Summary

Anne Applebaum and Tim Miller discuss the Trump administration’s foreign policy as a “globalized MAGA kleptocracy,” arguing that a significant faction sees itself as revolutionary rather than ordinary Republicans and is using U.S. power to build international allies, especially in Europe and the Western Hemisphere, while partnering with tech, cryptocurrency, oil, gas, minerals, and hedge-fund interests. They claim the State Department’s traditional soft-power tools—exchange programs, cultural initiatives, microgrants, and public diplomacy—are being repurposed to support far-right or MAGA-aligned actors, citing Under Secretary of State for Public Diplomacy Sarah Rogers’s statement about using “all the tools at our disposal” to advance an America-first agenda. The conversation covers the White House ballroom controversy, with Applebaum saying the project imposes Trump’s personality on a building originally meant to embody modest republican values, and references reporting by Ben Terris for New York Magazine. They describe Greenland as a case where private U.S. actors, not just diplomats, are pursuing influence: a Greenlandic politician’s wife was involved in U.S. negotiations, and he reportedly received a house paid for by American investors including Ron Lauder; Ken Griffin, Elon Musk, Dryden Brown, Palantir, Peter Thiel, and Sam Altman are also mentioned in connection with Greenland, crypto-backed “network state” ideas, or related influence projects. They discuss Brazil, where Trump-era tariffs, visa sanctions against Brazilian judges and officials, and pressure tied to Elon Musk’s anger over Twitter fines allegedly aimed to help the Bolsonaro family return to power, alongside a brief meeting between Trump and Lula brokered by a Brazilian meatpacking billionaire. On Venezuela, they cite Trump’s claim that the U.S. is taking “billions and billions” from Venezuelan assets, Treasury testimony that the U.S. controls sales of many Venezuelan assets, money moved from Qatar to a U.S. account reportedly at Citibank, no audit of inflows or outflows, and a deal involving Alejandro Betancourt, a Venezuelan oligarch reportedly under investigation in multiple countries; Harvard economist Ricardo Hausmann is quoted describing it as “a union of two corrupt oligarchies.” They also mention Venezuela’s repressive apparatus remaining intact, Delcy Rodriguez’s unelected status, political prisoner Perkins Rocha still under house arrest with an ankle bracelet, and Maria Corina Machado’s inability to return. The discussion turns toward Russia/Ukraine and Russian-linked figures, including Umar Kremlev, whom ProPublica reported funded a Don Jr. wedding-related party attended by Jared Kushner and others, though Bettina Trump denies the claim. Ad breaks included EasyCater’s workplace catering platform, a Dupixent/Dupixint ad for eosinophilic esophagitis, ZipRecruiter hiring services, and a traveling “Donald J. Trump and Jeffrey Epstein Memorial Reading Room” exhibit containing 3.5 million released Epstein files bound into 3,437 volumes and weighing about 17,000 pounds.
