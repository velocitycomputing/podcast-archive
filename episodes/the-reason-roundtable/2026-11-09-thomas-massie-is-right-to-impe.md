## Summary

Thomas Massie introduced a 34-page resolution to impeach Pete Hegseth for high crimes and misdemeanors tied to the Iran war, and said he would try to force a vote within days; the Reason Roundtable panel—Peter Suderman, Nick Gillespie, Matt Welch, and Catherine Renee Ward—debated whether impeachment is justified, with Gillespie arguing Hegseth’s specific orders in Gulf of Mexico/Gulf of America killings of roughly 240 people and “no quarter” instructions are distinct from Trump’s broader War Powers Act violations. The discussion also covered Bloomberg reporting that Democrats may shut down the government to stop the Iran war, Gene Healy’s call to impeach Hegseth, J.D. Vance’s anti-war positioning, Trump’s post-election personnel shake-up replacing Susie Wiles with Stephen Miller, Miller’s role in immigration enforcement, executive orders, “bomb the boats,” DOJ/FBI influence, habeas corpus skepticism, and white South African refugee preferences, plus WSJ reporting that Wiles was pushed out partly for not prosecuting leakers, reporters, and political opponents, and Maggie Haberman reporting that Miller favored winding down the Iran war. Panelists cited exit-poll claims that about seven in ten independents disliked Trump’s immigration enforcement, that Trump moved from plus-10 to minus-10 on immigration, that voters punished Trump on economy, corruption, and claims he made $2.5–$5 billion since the 2024 election, and they noted Democratic proposals from Bernie Sanders to expand Medicare to people 55+ and children/dental, Elizabeth Warren’s corporate investigations, Roe Conna’s DEI stance, the incoming Texas governor’s energy-price freeze, data-center regulation, a Nov. 10 Hook Hall debate with Mehdi Hassan and Rick Wilson on authoritarianism, and a Donor’s Trust ad warning donor-advised funds may redirect grants.

Actionable: track the Massie impeachment resolution’s text, articles, and floor vote; monitor whether Democrats attach Iran-war funding restrictions or shutdown threats to appropriations; and watch for legal challenges testing the 1973 War Powers Act. For research leads, read Gene Healy’s impeachment argument, Devlin Barrett’s “The Department of Revenge,” the Bloomberg shutdown report, the WSJ Wiles/Miller story, and Trump’s CBS/60 Minutes interview with Ross Douthett; follow-up questions should ask representatives whether they will vote to end or defund the Iran war, support impeachment articles tied to specific Gulf killings and no-quarter orders, oppose using DOJ/FBI for political prosecutions, and protect press/leaker rights. Health and personal-impact implications are indirect but concrete: if you are 55+, have children, or rely on dental/medical coverage, watch Medicare expansion proposals; if immigration enforcement affects you or family members, seek legal and medical support rather than relying on political promises; if you use a donor-advised fund, audit its grant policies and successor directions to ensure your money is not redirected to causes you oppose.
