## Summary

This episode of The AI Daily Brief opened with headlines. Anthropic said Claude, using a thousand agents over 21 hours and about 210 million tokens (roughly $10,000 of compute), found a novel CRISPR-like enzyme system, though its function is still unknown and biologists called the framing premature. At the UN, Trump rebranded AI as "super intelligence" and rejected a global regulator, even as 20 countries led by Finland called for mandatory safety testing and an international AI body. Altman and Amodei also addressed the Security Council, with Amodei pushing treaties, verification systems and global testing standards. The main segment covered whether consumers want personal agents at all. Ben Thompson and others argued people want to be entertained, not productive, and that shopping is fun. Others replied that demand is real for "schlep work" like forms, bookings and insurance calls. As evidence, Meta's Muse sits at #1 on the App Store. A New York Times writer called it the most useful AI app they had used, after it paid a parking ticket, made customer-service calls and negotiated a Facebook Marketplace purchase, though it required handing over very sensitive data. Meta also announced Muse on its glasses and a stopwatch-sized "Muse Charm" device due by the holidays. Grok Bot arrived inside Teslas for voice-driven ordering and email. The host closed by framing two things to watch: whether the move into the physical world drives adoption, and whether privacy concerns limit it.

For you, the practical step is to test a personal agent on narrow, tedious errands rather than on things you enjoy. Good candidates are filling out forms, paying tickets, booking appointments, canceling subscriptions and calling customer service. Weigh the privacy cost before connecting email, calendar and payment data, and require approval before payments, as Muse did in the NYT test. Treat the app-store rankings as weak evidence, since Meta's distribution can inflate them, and treat the Anthropic enzyme result as very preliminary. If you follow AI policy, the UN positions (US against a global regulator, Anthropic for one, OpenAI in between) are worth tracking. The host also asked listeners to take the fall listener survey at aidailybrief.ai.
