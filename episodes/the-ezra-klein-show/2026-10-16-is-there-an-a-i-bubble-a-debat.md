## Summary

Paul Kedrosky argues AI is a bubble because asset prices have detached from economic reality—there's massive overbuilding (like railroads or electrification in prior eras) driven by abundant capital and competitive pressure, with trillions embedded across insurance, sovereign wealth funds, and private credit. The S&P 500's top 10 stocks represent 40% of the index; CAPE ratios sit at 1929 crash levels; 50% of Americans' net worth is in equities concentrated in AI. Token prices are falling 13-fold annually, and early demand comes from atypical users (coders) whose spending doesn't represent real business adoption. Azeem Azhar counters that enterprise demand is genuine and growing, compute is undersupplied (not overbuilt), and hyperscalers are achieving payback fast enough that capex will monetize even if Anthropic or OpenAI collapse—the infrastructure itself has value independent of any single company's business model.

**Actionable:** If you hold S&P 500 index funds, you're heavily long AI through the top 10 stocks, meaning your retirement portfolio is concentrated in AI sentiment risk. Watch three signals: whether token prices keep falling faster than capability improves (suggesting demand weakness), whether hyperscalers' capex actually generates returns within 18–24 months (if timelines extend, cash drought hits), and the IPO lockup expirations for Anthropic and OpenAI next year (insiders will be selling into the market, testing real demand). A significant miss on either capex returns or enterprise adoption could trigger repricing across tech. Neither debater thinks AI is useless; the disagreement is whether trillions in spending are properly timed or front-loaded into a valley of actual use.
