## Summary

The episode explores why U.S. Treasury yields have risen to levels unseen since before the Great Recession, and what the Trump administration is attempting to do about it. Robin Wigglesworth discusses how these rising yields ripple through the economy—making mortgages, car loans, and credit cards more expensive while pressuring the stock market. The conversation centers on three core questions: the drivers behind the yield increases, the administration's attempts to reverse them (characterized as "erratic and futile"), and the economic consequences if the trend continues.

For personal financial planning, rising Treasury yields directly affect your borrowing costs across all major categories—if you're considering a mortgage, refinancing a car loan, or carrying credit card debt, delays now could save substantial money before rates rise further. Monitor mortgage rates and refinancing windows closely, and reassess your investment portfolio's exposure to rate-sensitive assets like bonds and growth stocks, which typically underperform when yields climb. The episode suggests uncertainty around policy effectiveness, making this an opportune time to lock in favorable rates if you have planned borrowing needs.

## Transcript

A lot of things are on track to get more expensive.
The U.S. Treasury market is the bedrock of the global economy. When yields on those Treasuries go up, mortgages, car loans and credit cards get more expensive, and it can hit the stock market, too. And yields have been going up – to levels we haven’t seen consistently since before the Great Recession – inspiring some erratic and futile efforts from the Trump administration to push them back down.
So why are yields creeping higher? What is the administration trying to do about it? And if this continues, what’s in store for the economy?
Robin Wigglesworth is the editor of the Financial Times blog Alphaville, a host of the podcast “The Story of Money” and the author of the forthcoming book “A Fabulous Debt: The Epic Story of How Bonds Built the Modern World.”
