## Summary

The episode featured three political experts—Matthew Yglesias (founder of Slow Boring newsletter), Claire Ainsley (Director of Center-Left Renewal at the Progressive Policy Institute), and Yascha Mounk—debating whether progressives have abandoned working-class voters and why center-left parties are losing ground across democracies. They examined whether these losses result from structural forces or strategic failures, analyzing lessons from center-left movements in Canada, Australia, and the UK. The discussion centered on tensions between progressive movements and median voters, and the role patriotism plays in electoral success.

For policy strategists, party operatives, and those studying electoral coalitions, this episode provides direct analysis from experts with hands-on experience in center-left governance and strategy. Ainsley's involvement in Keir Starmer's Labour government and Yglesias's focus on Democratic Party strategy offer concrete case studies for understanding what's failing in progressive outreach to working-class voters and what alternative approaches might work. The international examples provide actionable models for anyone designing or evaluating center-left political strategies.
