## Summary

Dr. Gayatri Devi discusses how dementia exists on a spectrum—encompassing Alzheimer's disease, vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia, and mixed presentations—with distinct but sometimes overlapping pathology involving amyloid, tau, and neuroinflammation. She explores how brain pathology doesn't always match symptoms and emphasizes detecting subtle cognitive decline in high-functioning patients through comprehensive evaluation. The episode covers blood-based biomarkers and APOE4 testing for asymptomatic risk assessment, the mechanisms and safety profile of anti-amyloid therapies (lecanemab and donanemab) including amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA), and her concept of menopause-related cognitive impairment in women. She also examines how individualized, multimodal treatment approaches—combining anti-amyloid drugs, GLP-1 agonists, MRI-guided TMS, and brain rehabilitation—can lead to stabilization or improvement in some patients, and discusses how AI and precision medicine are reshaping future dementia care.

If you're a woman approaching or in menopause experiencing cognitive changes, seek neurological evaluation rather than attributing it to normal aging, as hormone therapy and targeted rehabilitation may help. If you carry APOE4 variants or have a family history of dementia, blood biomarker screening is now accessible and can inform preventive decisions before symptoms appear. Anyone considering anti-amyloid therapy should understand ARIA risks and the importance of slow titration protocols to minimize complications. For those with existing cognitive decline, explore multimodal treatment strategies combining pharmacotherapy with lifestyle interventions (exercise, cognitive training) and emerging technologies like MRI-guided TMS, as individual responses vary significantly and some patients improve with personalized approaches.
