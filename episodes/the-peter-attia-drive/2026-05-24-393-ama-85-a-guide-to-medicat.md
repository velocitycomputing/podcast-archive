## Summary

Peter Attia's AMA #85 focuses on a systematic approach to evaluating medications and supplements by shifting from absolute judgments of "good" or "bad" to assessing whether a specific intervention makes sense for an individual's specific problem. The episode covers critical frameworks including the importance of clearly defining health problems before selecting interventions, how the intended purpose of a medication or supplement should determine the evidence standard required, the distinction between relative and absolute risk reduction, and why mechanistic reasoning alone is insufficient justification. Attia emphasizes that baseline risk dramatically shapes real-world benefit, that supplements require far greater skepticism than medications due to weaker US regulatory oversight, and that over-the-counter supplements face significant challenges including poor quality control, contamination, and unreliable efficacy claims.

Actionable takeaways include: first precisely define the health problem you're trying to solve; evaluate interventions using evidence hierarchies appropriate to their intended purpose; structure self-experiments to objectively test whether a supplement or medication actually works for you rather than relying on mechanistic reasoning; consider all downsides beyond side effects—cost, inconvenience, and opportunity cost matter; periodically reevaluate and discontinue therapies that aren't delivering meaningful benefit; and apply skepticism proportional to regulatory oversight (treat supplements with more caution than FDA-approved medications). Attia identifies only a small subset of over-the-counter supplements as having a reasonable risk-reward balance, making selective use and careful monitoring essential to avoiding wasted money and potential harm.
