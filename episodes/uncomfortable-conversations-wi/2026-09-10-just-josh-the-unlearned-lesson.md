## Summary

Josh Szeps recounts a pre-9/11 New York trip with his friend Joel, where they lie down in the World Trade Center courtyard and a passing finance worker joins them, then contrasts that memory with learning about the September 11, 2001 attacks from his parents’ home in Australia after his brother texts him. He claims the pre-9/11 West, especially the United States, regarded spectacular homeland mega-terrorism as movie-like and inconceivable, while counterterrorism professionals saw warning signs: Khalid Sheikh Mohammed and Ramzi Yousef’s 1995 Bojinka plot, the proposal to use aircraft as missiles, Osama bin Laden’s approval around 1998/1999, the USS Cole bombing, the 1998 Kenya/Tanzania embassy bombings, and a CIA President’s Daily Brief 36 days before 9/11 titled “Bin Laden Determined to Strike in US.” He names FBI counterterrorism expert John O’Neill, who resigned two weeks before the attacks over bureaucratic obstruction, became World Trade Center security chief, and died helping evacuate the South Tower. Szeps also cites late-night hosts David Letterman and Jon Stewart as capturing the shock, lists later attacks in Bali, Madrid, London, Paris, Nice, Berlin, and Manchester, and links 9/11 to the George W. Bush administration’s Cold War/state-centric blind spots, the first Gulf War’s 43-day campaign and 100-hour ground assault under George H.W. Bush and Colin Powell, Donald Rumsfeld’s earlier dealings with Saddam Hussein, and the claim that unrestrained power can appear invincible until it is deployed.
