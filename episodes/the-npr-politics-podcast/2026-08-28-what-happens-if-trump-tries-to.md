## Summary

The episode examines President Trump's pattern of undermining election credibility and explores how he aims to alter voting procedures ahead of the midterms, which begin in weeks. Hosted by NPR's Tamara Keith, Miles Parks, and Eric McDaniel, the discussion focuses on potential threats to election integrity specific to the midterm elections and details how Congressional Democrats are organizing their response to these threats.

This material is primarily informational rather than prescriptive—it surveys a political risk landscape rather than providing specific citizen actions. Listeners interested in election integrity should treat this as context for understanding the stakes, then follow up with their state and local election officials' guidance on how voting will actually work in their jurisdiction (often overlooked but the authoritative source), and consider whether to engage with Democratic organizing efforts if that aligns with their preferences.

## Transcript

President Trump routinely sows doubt in the credibility of elections in the United States — especially if he, or his allies, lose those votes. But with voting in this fall's midterms starting in just a few weeks, the president wants to change the ways Americans vote. We explore the risks to election integrity in these midterms and talk about how Congressional Democrats are preparing to respond.
This episode: senior political correspondent Tamara Keith, voting correspondent Miles Parks, and congressional reporter Eric McDaniel.
