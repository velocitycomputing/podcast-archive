## Summary

Jorma Kaukonen, founding Jefferson Airplane guitarist and Hot Tuna member, discussed his 85-year life and career, including his first song “Embryonic Journey,” which grew from Roger Perkins’s Drop D tuning, and his deep influence from Reverend Gary Davis, whose gospel-blues fingerstyle, complex counterpoint, and upbeat presence shaped Kaukonen’s playing. The interview covered his childhood in Karachi and Washington, D.C., anti-Semitism and bullying over his Finnish name, his first Gibson J-45 bought with help from classical guitarist Sophocles Pappas, and his Bay Area folk roots alongside Paul Kantner, Jerry Garcia, and Janis Joplin, whom he accompanied at hootenannies and whose early “Typewriter Tape” includes Kaukonen’s guitar. It also traced Jefferson Airplane’s formation, Kaukonen’s role in naming the band, the classic lineup with Grace Slick, Marty Balin, Spencer Dryden, Jack Casady, and Paul Kantner, and how Grace Slick brought “Somebody to Love” and “White Rabbit” from her previous band, the Great Society, into the Surrealistic Pillow arrangements. Key claims include Jerry Garcia’s credited “spiritual advisor” role on Surrealistic Pillow, Jack Casady’s bolero riff on “White Rabbit,” early recording limits of three- and four-track tape with no noise reduction, Kaukonen’s preference for locking with the rhythm section rather than front-stage soloing, his 1973/early-1970s departure from Jefferson Airplane because of formulaic music and infighting, his continued touring with Hot Tuna, his 1974 solo album Qua, his 2018 memoir Been So Long, his recovery from alcohol and drug addiction, his mixed view of LSD as cognitively illuminating but not musically helpful for him, and the 1965 archival release Wabash Avenue.
