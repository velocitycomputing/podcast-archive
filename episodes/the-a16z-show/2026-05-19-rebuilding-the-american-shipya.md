## Summary

The discussion centers on rebuilding the American defense industrial base for strategic competition, led by Michael Duffey (USDASD) and Dino Mavrookas. Key claims include shifting manufacturing toward speed, scale, and modern processes to overcome production capacity constraints; leveraging autonomy in both defense systems and industrial operations to reduce cost and complexity; building platforms designed for software and autonomy enabling new classes of vessels; and the necessity for structural DoD-industry reforms to streamline procurement. They emphasize commercial markets as critical support for defense capabilities, highlight the fragility of current supply chains, and stress that aligning private capital with national security priorities is essential for long-term resilience.

Follow-up questions should focus on specific examples of cost reduction through autonomy and the feasibility of scaling commercial market integration into defense manufacturing.

## Transcript

Erin Price-Wright speaks with Michael Duffey and Dino Mavrookas about what it will take to rebuild the American defense industrial base for a new era of competition. As production capacity becomes a central constraint, they outline how the system must shift toward speed, scale, and modern manufacturing.
The conversation covers the role of autonomy in both defense systems and industrial processes, and how new approaches to design, labor, and production can dramatically reduce cost and complexity. Mavrookas explains how building for software and autonomy enables entirely new classes of platforms, while Duffey emphasizes the need for structural changes in how the Department of Defense works with industry.
They also discuss the importance of commercial markets in supporting defense capabilities, the fragility of existing supply chains, and why aligning private capital with national priorities is essential to long-term resilience.
