## Summary

Ben Horowitz and Gagan Biani announced the Horowitz Andreessen Academy, a new school designed for the AI era that prioritizes learning by building over traditional lectures. The academy is a residential program in San Francisco for high school students who already know they want to build things—starting with ~50 founders-minded students—and provides access to real-world work at major tech companies (Databricks, NVIDIA, Stripe) plus immersion in the Silicon Valley ecosystem. They argue that traditional education was designed for the industrial age and is misaligned with an AI-driven future; the academy replaces 5–8 hours of daily lectures with self-paced learning using AI tools, structured projects, and intentional social dynamics. Ben emphasizes this is the best time in history to be 18 because young people will intuitively understand AI capabilities better than experienced professionals, giving them an asymmetric advantage in problem-solving and startup creation.

For someone evaluating education or training pathways: if you're 18 and already building or entrepreneurial, the academy offers a concrete alternative to college that combines technical skill-building with people skills developed through osmosis in real companies and peer networks—you get credibility signals (the a16z brand), job pipeline access, and founder peer relationships in one place. More broadly, the model is exportable: the emphasis on project-based curriculum, proximity to practitioners, and measuring success by student outcomes (not alumni donations or institutional status) provides a template that existing educational institutions could adopt. If you're in hiring or building products for young people, expect rising demand for education models that build judgment and people skills, not just technical credentials.
