## Summary

Historian Timothy Snyder, recently returned from Kharkiv, explained that Ukraine's military situation is paradoxical: Ukrainian forces are mechanizing the front and preventing Russian breakthroughs, but Russia is systematically destroying Ukraine's economy and civilian infrastructure because the U.S. has cut direct military and financial aid. Snyder argues that the first 10–12 days of munitions expended in the Iran war could have won the Ukraine conflict entirely, but instead those resources were wasted pointlessly. He emphasized that Ukraine and Russia are now the world's only two modern fighting forces—Ukraine innovates, Russia copies at scale months later—which means continued conflict unintentionally trains a more lethal Russian military. He also stressed that U.S. policy is irreversibly encouraging China to invade Taiwan (since Ukraine's struggles suggest invasion is viable) and guaranteeing nuclear proliferation (because Ukraine surrendered nuclear weapons in 1994 for safety guarantees Russia violated, signaling to every other nation that possessing weapons is the only real deterrent).

The actionable insight is that U.S. support for Ukraine is not a costly sideshow—it is the cheapest way to deter both Russian and Chinese aggression simultaneously, and to discourage global nuclear proliferation. By instead cutting aid and deploying unvetted emissaries like Kushner and Witkoff who cannot articulate Ukrainian interests, the U.S. has forfeited diplomatic credibility, wasted defense resources on the Iran theater, and sent adversaries the message that America cannot sustain a commitment to defend a technologically sophisticated ally. Snyder's core claim: the Trump administration's dismissal of courage and sovereignty—treating statecraft as transactional dealmaking among oligarchs rather than defense of national interests—is undermining U.S. power.
