## Summary

**What was discussed:**
Three years after October 7, Israel enjoys unprecedented military strength but faces diplomatic isolation, with the US-Israel bipartisan consensus eroding as Americans increasingly sympathize with Palestinians over Israelis—a historic Gallup first. Aaron David Miller explains that Gaza remains dysfunctional and divided: Israel and Hamas hold fundamentally irreconcilable positions on security, governance, and reconstruction, and despite three years of military campaign, Hamas persists because it controls guns, aid access, and the black market while Palestinians direct their anger primarily at Israel rather than their own leaders. The upcoming October 27 Israeli elections will likely produce a more pragmatic government under Gadi Eisenot, but Miller argues this will yield transaction, not transformation, on Gaza, Lebanon, or Iran. Netanyahu and Trump together have damaged Israel's image globally and Jewish security abroad, while the broader Middle East remains fragmented, with only three functional regional powers—Israel, Turkey, and Iran—capable of projecting force; Arab states like Saudi Arabia, despite modernization, lack comparable strategic autonomy.

**What's actionable:**
Monitor the Israeli election results on October 27 and track whether a post-Netanyahu government shifts US-Israel relations from transactional to genuinely rehabilitative with Europe and Democratic voters—this signals whether Israel's diplomatic isolation deepens or begins to reverse. Watch how the US administration (particularly if it changes in 2028) recalibrates Middle East strategy around Israel's true military capacity as a means to political ends, not an end in itself. Pay attention to Iran's regional staying power and the three-way Israeli-Turkish-Iranian power dynamic, as these will define regional stability far more than Arab state initiatives. Track whether US military support to Israel—currently flowing despite bipartisan erosion—remains sticky or becomes conditional, which would represent a fundamental shift in the core US alliance structure.
