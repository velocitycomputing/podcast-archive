## Summary

Marieke Flament (a computer engineer who worked at Circle, ran the UK digital neobank Metal, led a layer-one blockchain protocol, and now advises Kalis, N26 and IG Group) and Nicolas Colin (head of research at the deep-tech fund VSquare, ex-French finance ministry) explain how their newsletter Currency of Power began as "Euro Stable Watch", a niche focus on euro stablecoins, then broadened into the "reinvention of money". They argue that the useful core of "crypto" is peer-to-peer value transfer plus smart contracts, which make money programmable. In their example, an SPV's legal waterfall (carry, expense reimbursement, shareholder splits) would execute automatically when cash arrives, instead of a manager computing it by hand and instructing a bank. Colin concedes that decentralized systems tend to recentralize under large players. Flament expects the roughly 150 blockchains to consolidate to a few winners (Ethereum and Solana for open use, plus payment-specific or bank-oriented chains). The central thesis comes from Carlota Perez's technological-revolutions framework. Colin says we are in the maturity phase of the semiconductor, computing and networks paradigm. The previous maturity phase, the 1970s, produced a financial reset: the Nixon shock, junk bonds, leveraged buyouts and KKR, the Bloomberg terminal, Vanguard and passive investing, "Mayday" in 1975 and London's Big Bang. They therefore expect a new financial system to emerge now, which they call "the great financial reset". On why Bretton Woods ended, they cite Martin Wolf's view that the US would not devalue after Vietnam and Great Society spending. They also cite Mac Green's argument that a hard-asset anchor is inelastic and pushes up borrowing costs, which constrains growth. The excerpt is truncated before it reaches the 2026 market events or any concrete conclusions.

What's actionable is mostly a framework to apply rather than specific trades, because the supplied portion never reaches positions or recommendations. First, treat tokenization and stablecoins as financial plumbing: track real use cases such as automated payouts, tokenized stocks and gold, and bank adoption of Ethereum or Solana-type infrastructure, not coin prices. Second, use Perez's "maturity phase" lens. If the 1970s analogy holds, expect innovation in capital-raising, new instruments, regulation and market structure, and watch for regulatory inflection points (the modern equivalents of Mayday and Big Bang). Third, note that euro stablecoins are a Europe-sovereignty issue, since nearly all stablecoins are dollar-denominated. You could follow their Currency of Power newsletter and podcast, and read the article "The Great Financial Reset Has Begun". Treat the Perez analogy as a thesis, not a settled fact. The summary above does not test the claim that the 1970s pattern repeats, and the hosts' own stablecoin and crypto involvement means they have a stake in the outcome.
