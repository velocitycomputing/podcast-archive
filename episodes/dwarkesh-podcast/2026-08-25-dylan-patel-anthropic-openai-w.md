## Summary

Dylan Patel and host discussed how lab economics are shifting from inference-heavy workloads to training as the field approaches recursive self-improvement, with Anthropic and OpenAI positioned to control most of the world's usable compute by 2028 due to superior monetization and outbidding capabilities. The conversation explored compute pricing dynamics, whether the >$10 trillion in AI capex expected by decade's end could trigger a sovereign debt crisis (where hyperscaler borrowing raises interest rates and bankrupts non-AI-exposed countries), and the seemingly unstoppable centralization forces—economies of scale in training, compute scarcity, and continual learning—that concentrate AI power in a handful of companies.

For investors and strategists, compute becomes the bottleneck and primary battleground; if Anthropic and OpenAI control most usable FLOPs, they'll capture disproportionate surplus value and determine who accesses frontier AI capabilities. This threatens sovereign wealth and equity valuations in non-AI sectors if the macro predictions hold. For individuals, employment and economic value increasingly flow to companies that own compute, making positioning in or near AI-dominant firms critical. The unresolved question—whether anything can counter this centralization—suggests the trend will persist absent major intervention.
