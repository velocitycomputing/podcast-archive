## Summary

Steve Keen argues the global economy is being reshaped by a “perfect storm” of wars, the aftermath of the global financial crisis caused by excessive private debt rather than government debt, the epochal shift of manufacturing from the West to China, and global warming, with El Niño adding 0.2–0.4°C rapidly, pushing global average temperature from about 15.5°C toward 15.7–15.9°C, or 1.5–1.9°C above pre-fossil-fuel levels, after about 90% of warming heat entered the ocean. He claims neoliberal policy since the 1970s shifted macroeconomic management from low unemployment to low inflation, failed to deliver promised growth, and reduced per-capita income growth from roughly 3% annually in 1945–1975 to about 1% since 1975, while de-unionization, gig work, privatized education/health, and rising private debt shifted income from workers to bankers/rentiers; he cites Marx, Adam Smith, Joseph Schumpeter, Reagan, Thatcher, Bill Clinton, Elon Musk, Microsoft, Google, Kingston University, University of Western Sydney, Paul Fritters, and Andy Burnham, with anecdotes including a $12,500 house costing one year’s income then versus ten years now, his £88,000 UK salary leaving about £2,000/month to live on and making him about 40% worse off than in Australia, UK minimum-wage increases around £27,000/year costing a small pie shop about £700/week, and AI/data-center investment estimated at 2–3% of GDP with overinvestment possibly five times income, a bubble he expects to burst in under a year, potentially leaving only about 10% of firms surviving, analogous to 1990s telecom dark fibre where 10x cable was laid and 90% was unused. The transcript also includes a Function Health advertisement about testosterone/hormone testing, 160+ lab tests, clinician review, and $365/year or $340 with code.

For the user, the actionable takeaways are to treat housing, AI, and macroeconomic narratives as leverage-sensitive bets: check personal household-debt-to-income and mortgage affordability against local wage growth rather than assuming house prices keep rising; stress-test career exposure to AI by identifying whether work is primarily clerical/research/robotics-substitutable and build skills in physical, regulated, creative, or human-trust domains; monitor concrete indicators such as private credit growth, wage share of GDP, data-center capex versus revenue, GPU/copper demand, semiconductor supply, and UK/Australia cost-of-living comparisons if considering relocation; if the Function Health segment is relevant, consider whether baseline hormone/lab testing is worth discussing with a clinician, especially for symptoms of low testosterone or unexplained fatigue, but verify test validity, cost, and follow-up care rather than treating the ad as medical advice; useful follow-up questions are whether Keen’s AI-bubble timeline has a measurable trigger, how much AI capex is debt-financed, whether El Niño’s 0.2–0.4°C bump is temporary, whether private debt or asset-price inflation is driving housing, and whether policy responses such as universal high income, public provisioning, or reindustrialization are realistic.
