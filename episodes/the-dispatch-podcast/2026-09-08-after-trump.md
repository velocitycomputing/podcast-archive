## Summary

This Dispatch roundtable, hosted by Steve Hayes with Kevin Williamson, Michael Warren (the transcript renders the name "Renault") and Jonah Goldberg, was recorded on Labor Day, 8 weeks before the November 3, 2026 midterms. It previews the Dispatch's annual editorial on the "post-Trump reality." Hayes cites the following as evidence of a likely Republican wipeout: Nate Silver gives the GOP a "low probability" of keeping the House, Democrats lead 9 of 11 competitive Senate races in his polling snapshot, and the NYT reports a 10-point Democratic party-ID advantage. He also points to blame for high prices, an unpopular Iran war and record-high perceived corruption.

Williamson says a decisive loss is what Republicans need, and he hopes every Republican candidate loses. He argues the party is no longer the limited-government option because of Trump's equity stakes in companies, deficits and the 2021 attempt to overturn the election. He is also wary that Democrats will read a win as license to follow their DSA wing.

Goldberg rejects the "burn it all down" framing as unrealistic. He argues Trump has blurred party lines on statism (about 32 corporate equity stakes, a subsidized rival oil company, a stay-at-home-mothers benefit he calls "UBI"), abortion, alliances and character. He also says catastrophizing ("lose and all is lost") is anti-American, since there is always another election. He points to Susan Collins and Jon Ossoff as models of governing like a majority party through constituent service.

Warren says the editorial is timed for now because post-election conversations are already starting. He cites the $40 trillion debt and weak US leadership abroad as problems neither party is taking seriously. The supplied transcript cuts off mid-discussion, so the Witkoff–Putin segment and the "Not Worth Your Time" jobs segment aren't covered.

For you, the practical step is to read the Dispatch editorial that went out the day after this recording, because it is the fuller statement of the argument. The episode's own list of things to watch is the Texas Senate race, where a Republican loss is Williamson's test of whether the GOP sobers up, plus the Collins (Maine) and Ossoff (Georgia) races. Early voting was due to start within weeks of the recording, so check your state's dates and deadlines now. This episode is a month old (today is October 9), so compare its polling claims (Silver's House and Senate odds, the Gallup corruption figure) against current numbers before relying on them. If you hold or advise on market-sensitive positions, the discussion points to two themes: continued fiscal risk from the debt, and policy volatility from state-directed corporate involvement, whichever party wins. Expect the first couple of weeks after the election to bring denial-style spin from Republicans, and treat early post-election statements as a poor guide to whether the party actually changes course.
