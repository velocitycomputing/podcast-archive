## Summary

President Trump’s net approval and the GOP’s 2026 midterm prospects were the central topics in a Dispatch roundtable hosted by Steve Hayes with Jonah Goldberg, Mike Warren, and John McCormack, covering roughly two months to the election, a couple dozen competitive House races, and six to eight competitive Senate races. They cited Real Clear Politics averages of -16.7 overall, -26.7 on the economy, -39.1 on inflation, -19.9 on foreign policy, -26.5 on Iran, and -9.8 on immigration, plus a 24-point wrong-track gap and inflation approval of 29% approve versus 68.1% disapprove, while noting Trump’s Rose Garden cheeseburger dinner with Republicans, his Super PAC’s two Trump-only ads, and his promise to campaign in battleground states. The panel debated whether Trump-centered campaigning is rational because he remains the GOP’s most popular figure and could motivate unmotivated Republican voters, or ineffective because his approval is in the high 30s, economic approval is roughly two-to-one negative, tariffs and inflation contradict voters’ experiences, egg prices fell but other grocery items were flat or up, and 2024 promises on prices and Iran have not matched reality; they also described one ad as policy-focused on no tax on tips and Social Security and the other as personality-focused, with Goldberg suggesting advisers may have packaged the policy ad to get Trump’s approval while preferring personality ads. Warren and McCormack argued Trump’s ego, spending on safe seats such as Darlene Graham rather than competitive Texas Senate races, and the lack of other congressional accomplishments make a Trump-centered strategy weak, while Goldberg said Trump’s weakness is not with MAGA voters but with conservative-leaning independents, Hispanic voters, and normie Republicans; Warren’s Next Right reporting highlighted Trump’s crumbling support among Hispanic voters in three redrawn South Texas Rio Grande districts that Trump won in 2024 but where all GOP House candidates trail Democrats, including Monica De La Cruz down about three points to Bobby Pledo, with Trump’s approval underwater and Gov. Greg Abbott also down.
