## Summary

SpaceX's potential IPO was analyzed through multiple levers: Starship rapid reusability, Starlink broadband and direct-to-cell services, Elon Musk's emerging AI compute business (xAI), and the Cursor acquisition. The panel debated whether SpaceX could become "Elon Web Services," a new AI hyperscaler, citing that SpaceX's AI compute business reportedly has the "highest operating profit per gigawatt" among major players. Key economic data included orbital data centers costing $5B per gigawatt versus $25B on the ground, and the group questioned if SpaceX could achieve an 8X revenue increase in four years. The discussion also covered the AI model landscape (Fable 5, Mythos, ChatGPT 5.5), arguing that 90% of AI revenue accrues to frontier models, while the AI CapEx boom ($1.5T vs. $300B in AI revenue) may indicate market underestimation of demand scale.

Investors should assess orbital data center economics ($5B/gigawatt vs. $25B on ground) as a potential catalyst for SpaceX's AI compute dominance, questioning if "Elon Web Services" can scale profitably. Evaluate the $1.5T AI CapEx versus $300B revenue math to gauge if current infrastructure spending is sustainable or overhyped. Scrutinize SpaceX's post-IPO structure (lock-ups, drawdowns) when sizing positions, and consider how acquisitions like Cursor directly impact xAI's model development. Follow up on whether frontier AI models (like Fable 5) will maintain revenue dominance over open source, and whether Nvidia's dominance faces real ASIC competition.

## Transcript

Brad Gerstner sits down with Gavin Baker and Andrew Fox of Atreides Management, alongside Altimeter partner Clark Tang, to break down one of the biggest questions in tech and markets: how should investors think about the SpaceX IPO?
They unpack the major levers behind SpaceX’s next phase: Starship rapid reusability, Starlink broadband and direct-to-cell, Elon’s emerging AI compute business, xAI’s model ambitions, the Cursor acquisition, and the long-term promise of orbital data centers.
The group also debates whether SpaceX is becoming a new kind of AI hyperscaler — “Elon Web Services” — and what that means for the future of compute, cloud, and frontier intelligence.
Then they dive into the latest model race: Fable 5, Mythos, ChatGPT 5.5, long-running agents, open source vs. frontier models, Nvidia vs. ASICs, the AI CapEx boom, and why the market may still be underestimating the scale of AI demand. Enjoy another episode of BG2!
